SECTION 1: WHAT IS DIABETES?
================================================================================

Diabetes Mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood 
glucose (hyperglycemia) resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, 
or both.

INSULIN: A hormone produced by beta cells of the pancreatic islets of Langerhans.
- Allows cells to absorb glucose from the blood for energy.
- Signals the liver to store glucose as glycogen.
- Without adequate insulin or insulin action, glucose builds up in the blood.

NORMAL BLOOD GLUCOSE VALUES:
- Fasting blood glucose: 70-99 mg/dL (3.9-5.5 mmol/L)
- 2-hour post-meal (postprandial): < 140 mg/dL (< 7.8 mmol/L)
- HbA1c (Glycated Hemoglobin): < 5.7%

PREDIABETES:
- Fasting glucose: 100-125 mg/dL
- 2-hour glucose: 140-199 mg/dL
- HbA1c: 5.7-6.4%
- Without intervention, 15-30% of prediabetics develop Type 2 Diabetes within 5 years.

DIAGNOSIS OF DIABETES (any one of the following):
- Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL (on two occasions)
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL during 75g OGTT
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5% (confirmed with repeat testing)
- Random plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL with symptoms

SECTION 2: TYPES OF DIABETES
================================================================================

TYPE 1 DIABETES MELLITUS (T1DM):
- Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells → absolute insulin deficiency.
- Body's immune system attacks insulin-producing cells.
- Requires lifelong insulin therapy.
- Onset: Usually childhood or young adulthood (but can occur at any age).
- Prevalence: ~5-10% of all diabetes cases.
- Not preventable (autoimmune origin).
- Risk factors: Genetic predisposition, possible viral triggers.
- Formerly called "juvenile diabetes" or "insulin-dependent diabetes."

TYPE 2 DIABETES MELLITUS (T2DM):
- Progressive insulin resistance + relative insulin deficiency.
- Most common form (90-95% of all diabetes).
- Strong link to obesity, physical inactivity, diet, genetics.
- Usually adult onset (but increasingly in young adults and adolescents).
- Initially treatable with lifestyle + oral medications; may eventually require insulin.
- Preventable and reversible in early stages with lifestyle modification.
- Risk factors: Obesity, family history, physical inactivity, poor diet, age > 45, gestational diabetes history.

GESTATIONAL DIABETES MELLITUS (GDM):
- Diabetes diagnosed during pregnancy (usually 2nd or 3rd trimester).
- Results from pregnancy hormones causing insulin resistance.
- Affects 7-10% of pregnancies.
- Usually resolves after delivery.
- Increases risk of developing Type 2 Diabetes later in life.
- Risks for baby: Macrosomia (large baby), neonatal hypoglycemia, preterm birth.
- Screening: OGTT (75g or 100g) at 24-28 weeks gestation.

OTHER SPECIFIC TYPES:
- MODY (Maturity-Onset Diabetes of the Young): Genetic forms of diabetes.
- Latent Autoimmune Diabetes in Adults (LADA): Slow-onset T1DM in adults (Type 1.5).
- Secondary diabetes: From pancreatitis, Cushing's syndrome, acromegaly, medications (steroids).

SECTION 3: SYMPTOMS OF DIABETES
================================================================================

CLASSIC SYMPTOMS ("3 Polys" + 1):
1. POLYURIA: Frequent, excessive urination (due to glucose spilling in urine, drawing water with it - osmotic diuresis).
2. POLYDIPSIA: Excessive thirst (due to dehydration from polyuria).
3. POLYPHAGIA: Excessive hunger (cells can't use glucose, brain signals hunger).
4. UNEXPLAINED WEIGHT LOSS: Despite eating more (especially in Type 1 - body burns fat and muscle for energy).

ADDITIONAL SYMPTOMS:
- Fatigue and weakness (cells not getting glucose for energy)
- Blurred vision (high glucose changes lens shape)
- Slow-healing wounds (especially on feet)
- Frequent infections (bacterial, fungal) - urinary tract, skin, yeast infections
- Tingling or numbness in hands and feet (early neuropathy)
- Dry, itchy skin
- Dark velvety skin patches in armpits, neck, groin (Acanthosis Nigricans - sign of insulin resistance)
- Erectile dysfunction in men
- Recurrent vaginal infections in women

SYMPTOMS IN TYPE 1 (Rapid Onset):
- Above symptoms develop quickly (days to weeks).
- May present with Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) as first manifestation:
  * Nausea, vomiting, severe abdominal pain.
  * Fruity breath (acetone).
  * Deep, rapid breathing (Kussmaul breathing).
  * Confusion, loss of consciousness.
  * MEDICAL EMERGENCY.

SYMPTOMS IN TYPE 2 (Often Silent):
- Many people have T2DM for years without symptoms.
- Often diagnosed on routine screening.
- May first present with complications (vision loss, kidney disease, heart attack).

SECTION 4: COMPLICATIONS OF DIABETES
================================================================================

ACUTE COMPLICATIONS:

1. DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS (DKA):
   - Mainly Type 1. Triggered by: infection, missed insulin, surgery.
   - Blood glucose typically > 250 mg/dL + ketones + acidosis (pH < 7.3).
   - Symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid breathing, fruity odor.
   - Treatment: IV fluids, insulin infusion, electrolyte replacement (especially potassium).
   - MEDICAL EMERGENCY - can be fatal.

2. HYPEROSMOLAR HYPERGLYCEMIC STATE (HHS):
   - Mainly Type 2. Extreme hyperglycemia (often > 600 mg/dL) without significant ketosis.
   - Severe dehydration, altered consciousness.
   - MEDICAL EMERGENCY.

3. HYPOGLYCEMIA (Low Blood Sugar):
   - Blood glucose < 70 mg/dL.
   - Causes: Too much insulin/medication, missed meal, excessive exercise.
   - Symptoms: Sweating, shaking, anxiety, hunger, palpitations (mild).
   - Severe: Confusion, seizures, unconsciousness.
   - Treatment: "15-15 Rule" - 15g of fast-acting carbohydrates, recheck in 15 minutes.
   - Fast-acting carbs: 3-4 glucose tablets, 4 oz fruit juice, regular (not diet) soda.
   - Severe/unconscious: IV dextrose (D50) or glucagon injection.

CHRONIC COMPLICATIONS (Long-term, from Chronic Hyperglycemia):

MICROVASCULAR (Small Blood Vessel Disease):
1. DIABETIC RETINOPATHY (Eye):
   - Damage to blood vessels in retina.
   - Leading cause of adult blindness in working-age population.
   - Stages: Background → Pre-proliferative → Proliferative.
   - Symptoms: Blurred vision, floaters, sudden vision loss.
   - Screening: Annual dilated eye exam.
   - Treatment: Laser photocoagulation, anti-VEGF injections, vitrectomy.

2. DIABETIC NEPHROPATHY (Kidney):
   - Damage to kidney filtering units (glomeruli).
   - Leading cause of end-stage renal disease (dialysis).
   - Signs: Microalbuminuria → Macroalbuminuria → Declining GFR → Kidney failure.
   - Screening: Annual urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) and eGFR.
   - Treatment: ACE inhibitors, ARBs, SGLT2 inhibitors, blood pressure control.

3. DIABETIC NEUROPATHY (Nerves):
   - Most common complication of diabetes.
   - Types:
     * Peripheral: Numbness, tingling, burning in feet and hands ("glove and stocking" pattern).
     * Autonomic: Affects heart rate, blood pressure, digestion, bladder, sexual function.
     * Focal/Mononeuropathy: Sudden weakness of one nerve.
   - Treatment: Good glucose control, pain medications (gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine).
   - Diabetic foot care is critical to prevent ulcers and amputation.

MACROVASCULAR (Large Blood Vessel Disease):
1. CORONARY ARTERY DISEASE (Heart):
   - Diabetics have 2-4x higher risk of heart attack.
   - Heart disease is the leading cause of death in diabetics.
   - May be "silent" (no chest pain due to autonomic neuropathy).

2. STROKE:
   - Diabetics have 2-4x higher risk of stroke.

3. PERIPHERAL ARTERIAL DISEASE (Legs):
   - Reduced blood flow to legs.
   - Causes: Leg pain when walking (claudication), poor wound healing, gangrene.
   - Contributes significantly to lower limb amputations.

DIABETIC FOOT:
- Combination of neuropathy (no pain sensation) + poor circulation + infection.
- Small cuts, blisters, calluses → unnoticed → infected ulcers → osteomyelitis → amputation.
- PREVENTION: Daily foot inspection, proper footwear, no barefoot walking, podiatrist visits.

SECTION 5: TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT OF DIABETES
================================================================================

TREATMENT GOALS:
- HbA1c: < 7% for most diabetics (lower for young, higher for elderly/frail).
- Fasting glucose: 80-130 mg/dL.
- Post-meal glucose: < 180 mg/dL.
- Blood pressure: < 130/80 mmHg.
- LDL cholesterol: < 70 mg/dL (< 55 mg/dL in very high cardiovascular risk).

A. LIFESTYLE MANAGEMENT (Foundation of Treatment):

MEDICAL NUTRITION THERAPY (Diet):
- Carbohydrates: Focus on quality (whole grains, vegetables, legumes) over quantity.
- Limit: Refined carbohydrates (white rice, white bread, sugary drinks), saturated fat, sodium.
- Increase: Fiber (slows glucose absorption), lean protein, healthy fats.
- The "Plate Method": Half plate non-starchy vegetables, quarter lean protein, quarter whole grains.
- Calorie restriction for overweight/obese T2DM patients.
- Alcohol: In moderation only; never drink on empty stomach (hypoglycemia risk).

PHYSICAL ACTIVITY:
- 150 minutes/week of moderate aerobic exercise (brisk walking, cycling, swimming).
- Resistance training 2-3 times/week.
- Breaks sedentary time every 30 minutes.
- Exercise lowers blood glucose by increasing insulin sensitivity.
- Caution: Check blood glucose before exercise if on insulin.

WEIGHT MANAGEMENT:
- Even 5-10% weight loss significantly improves blood sugar control in T2DM.
- Bariatric surgery: For BMI > 35 with uncontrolled T2DM; can achieve remission.

SMOKING CESSATION:
- Smoking worsens cardiovascular risk, increases complications.

B. ORAL MEDICATIONS (Type 2 Diabetes):

1. METFORMIN (First-line):
   - Mechanism: Reduces glucose production in liver (hepatic gluconeogenesis).
   - Also improves insulin sensitivity.
   - Does not cause weight gain or hypoglycemia.
   - Cardiovascular protective effects.
   - Side effects: GI upset (nausea, diarrhea) - take with food; reduce with extended-release form.
   - Contraindications: eGFR < 30 mL/min, IV contrast dye use, severe liver disease, alcoholism.
   - Rarely: Lactic acidosis (serious but very rare).

2. SGLT2 INHIBITORS (Sodium-Glucose Co-transporter 2):
   - Drugs: Empagliflozin (Jardiance), Dapagliflozin (Farxiga), Canagliflozin (Invokana).
   - Mechanism: Block glucose reabsorption in kidneys → glucose excreted in urine.
   - Additional benefits: Reduce weight, blood pressure, cardiovascular events, kidney disease progression.
   - Side effects: Urinary tract infections, genital fungal infections, DKA (rare).

3. GLP-1 RECEPTOR AGONISTS:
   - Drugs: Semaglutide (Ozempic - injectable, Rybelsus - oral), Liraglutide (Victoza), Dulaglutide (Trulicity).
   - Mechanism: Stimulate insulin secretion, suppress glucagon, slow gastric emptying, suppress appetite.
   - Major benefits: Weight loss (significant), cardiovascular risk reduction.
   - Side effects: Nausea, vomiting (usually transient), pancreatitis (rare).

4. DPP-4 INHIBITORS (Gliptins):
   - Drugs: Sitagliptin (Januvia), Saxagliptin, Vildagliptin, Teneligliptin.
   - Mechanism: Inhibit DPP-4 enzyme → more GLP-1 available → more insulin.
   - Weight neutral. Kidney-safe (dose adjust in CKD).
   - Side effects: Nasopharyngitis, pancreatitis (rare), heart failure risk (saxagliptin).

5. SULFONYLUREAS:
   - Drugs: Glibenclamide, Glipizide, Glimepiride, Gliclazide.
   - Mechanism: Stimulate insulin secretion from beta cells (regardless of glucose level).
   - Risk of hypoglycemia. Weight gain.
   - Cheap and effective but older class.

6. THIAZOLIDINEDIONES (TZDs / Glitazones):
   - Drugs: Pioglitazone.
   - Mechanism: Increase insulin sensitivity in peripheral tissues.
   - Reduces liver fat, may help NAFLD.
   - Side effects: Weight gain, fluid retention, heart failure risk, fractures, bladder cancer risk.

7. ALPHA-GLUCOSIDASE INHIBITORS:
   - Drugs: Acarbose, Voglibose.
   - Mechanism: Slow carbohydrate digestion → reduce post-meal glucose spikes.
   - Must take with first bite of each meal.
   - Side effects: Flatulence, diarrhea, abdominal bloating.

C. INSULIN THERAPY:

TYPES OF INSULIN:
1. Rapid-Acting: Lispro (Humalog), Aspart (NovoLog), Glulisine. Onset: 15 min. Peak: 1-2 hrs. Duration: 3-5 hrs.
2. Short-Acting (Regular): Onset: 30-60 min. Peak: 2-4 hrs. Duration: 5-8 hrs. (Used IV in hospital).
3. Intermediate-Acting (NPH): Onset: 2-4 hrs. Duration: 12-18 hrs.
4. Long-Acting: Glargine (Lantus), Detemir (Levemir), Degludec (Tresiba). Duration: 20-24+ hrs. No peak.
5. Pre-Mixed: Combination of short + intermediate acting.

INSULIN REGIMENS:
- Basal only: One injection of long-acting insulin daily (for T2DM starting insulin).
- Basal-Bolus: Long-acting once daily + rapid-acting with each meal (for T1DM and advanced T2DM).
- Insulin pump (CSII): Continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion. For T1DM.

INSULIN INJECTION SITES: Abdomen (fastest absorption), thigh (slowest), upper arm, buttocks.

SECTION 6: MONITORING DIABETES
================================================================================

SELF-MONITORING OF BLOOD GLUCOSE (SMBG):
- Using a glucometer at home.
- Type 1: Test before meals and bedtime; more frequently during illness, exercise changes.
- Type 2 on insulin: Test before meals and bedtime.
- Type 2 on oral meds: As advised by doctor.

CONTINUOUS GLUCOSE MONITORING (CGM):
- Sensor inserted under skin measures glucose every 1-5 minutes.
- Devices: FreeStyle Libre (Abbott), Dexterity (Dexcom), Medtronic.
- Time-in-Range (TIR): Goal > 70% of readings between 70-180 mg/dL.

HbA1c:
- Reflects average blood glucose over past 2-3 months.
- Check every 3 months initially, then every 6 months when stable.
- Target: < 7% for most patients.
- A1c of 7% corresponds to average glucose of ~154 mg/dL.

URINE KETONES:
- Test during illness or if blood glucose > 250 mg/dL (especially T1DM).

SECTION 7: PRECAUTIONS FOR DIABETICS
================================================================================

DIET PRECAUTIONS:
1. Never skip meals (especially if on insulin or sulfonylureas - hypoglycemia risk).
2. Count carbohydrates carefully.
3. Avoid sugar-sweetened beverages, fruit juices, candy.
4. Use sugar alternatives (stevia, sucralose) in moderation.
5. Read food labels carefully - watch for hidden sugars.
6. Limit sodium to < 2300 mg/day (blood pressure control).
7. When eating out: Choose grilled over fried; choose whole grain options; ask for dressings on the side.

FOOT CARE PRECAUTIONS (Critical):
1. Inspect feet daily for cuts, blisters, redness, swelling, sores.
2. Wash feet daily in lukewarm water; dry completely between toes.
3. Moisturize feet (but NOT between toes).
4. NEVER walk barefoot - indoors or outdoors.
5. Trim toenails straight across; file edges.
6. Wear well-fitting shoes; check inside shoes for objects before wearing.
7. Wear comfortable cotton/wool socks without tight elastic.
8. Do not use heating pads/hot water bottles (burn risk due to neuropathy).
9. See a podiatrist regularly.
10. Report any foot wound to doctor immediately - even small ones.

SICK DAY RULES:
1. Never stop insulin during illness (need may increase).
2. Continue oral medications (unless instructed otherwise).
3. Monitor blood glucose every 2-4 hours.
4. Check urine or blood ketones frequently.
5. Stay hydrated (sip fluids every 15-30 minutes).
6. Eat soft, bland foods if can't eat normally.
7. Contact doctor if: Blood glucose > 300 mg/dL, ketones positive, can't keep fluids down, confused.

EXERCISE PRECAUTIONS:
1. Check blood glucose before exercise.
2. If < 100 mg/dL, eat a small snack before exercising.
3. Carry fast-acting sugar (glucose tablets, juice) during exercise.
4. Avoid exercise if blood glucose > 250 mg/dL with ketones.
5. Wear proper footwear.
6. Stay hydrated.

TRAVEL PRECAUTIONS:
1. Carry twice the medication needed (in carry-on, NOT checked baggage).
2. Keep insulin cool; do not freeze.
3. Carry medical ID (bracelet or card) indicating diabetic.
4. Carry snacks to prevent hypoglycemia.
5. Time zone changes: Consult doctor about insulin timing.

MEDICATION SAFETY:
1. Medications that raise blood glucose: Steroids, thiazide diuretics, beta-blockers, antipsychotics, certain antiretrovirals.
2. Medications that lower blood glucose (hypoglycemia risk with antidiabetics): Alcohol, aspirin (high dose), certain antibiotics.

SECTION 8: DIABETES AND SPECIAL SITUATIONS
================================================================================

DIABETES AND SURGERY:
- Blood glucose must be well-controlled before elective surgery.
- High glucose increases infection risk, delays wound healing.
- Hold metformin 24-48 hours before surgery with contrast.
- Continuous glucose monitoring during surgery.

DIABETES AND PREGNANCY:
- Pre-gestational diabetes requires tight control (HbA1c < 6.5% ideally before conception).
- Increased risk of: Miscarriage, birth defects (especially neural tube, cardiac), pre-eclampsia, macrosomia.
- Insulin is the only approved medication for glucose control in pregnancy (most oral drugs not safe).

DIABETES AND RAMADAN FASTING:
- Major risk of hypoglycemia during fasting, hyperglycemia after Iftar.
- Consult doctor before Ramadan to adjust medications.
- SGLT2 inhibitors: Increased DKA risk during fasting.
- Many diabetics may safely fast with proper planning; some should not.

DIABETES AND COVID-19:
- Diabetics have higher risk of severe COVID-19 and complications.
- COVID-19 can cause new-onset diabetes (damage to pancreas).
- Uncontrolled glucose worsens COVID-19 outcomes.
